Oliver Hagen

Music

Mr Pineapple - Drawing Evil Eyebrows on Everything LP

A wonderful full-length album by Mr Pineapple. I'm on keys and BVs.

Background

I joined the good ship Mr Pineapple in early 2025, having first witnessed them at a gig in late 2023 where I instantly appreciated their mix of well-executed rock, prog and general silliness. Since then, we've played a bunch of gigs and recorded and released an EP called 'Live In The Shed'. Alex (lead vox and guitar) is the principal songwriter of the group. My addition is mainly textural, rounding out the lean guitar-bass-drums sound with keyboard parts that fill up the gaps. This has allowed the band to move into some pretty epic-sounding territory, which was showcased on the EP mainly on the track Cheesecake. Keyboard-wise, I've tried to push a little further in this direction on this album.

Composition

As mentioned above, Alex is the group's writer and nearly all the songs are entirely his; the only exceptions being Juice Overture, which is lots of his riffs and themes from across the album glued together by me, and Barry Evans Shows Janine His New Car Horn, which is an adventurous group improvisation based on a riff Alex provided. As with the previous EP, Alex sent us demos of each song to learn, with any important parts on each instrument played in and the rest left for us to fill out as we saw fit. Some of the tracks had a prescribed part pretty much the whole way through, and others had no keys part whatsoever, which gave me plenty of room to run around in.

As I mentioned above, the main contribution I seek to make to a Pineapple song is textural, filling out spaces and hopefully setting off and enhancing the other parts nicely. Occasionally however I will pop my head up and play something a little more elaborate or foreground-y, if I reckon the music demands it. It's important to me not to overstep and impose my musical inclinations over the direction the song wants to go in, so I try to wait for places where I absolutely can't help but hear a part in my head where these isn't one elsewhere. There are a few such places on this album:

The Little Billy Chronicles

The demo came over with the ending epic Mellotron-style strings already on it, but no other keyboard parts. I was in Cornwall on holiday with my family at the time I first heard this, and I'd bought my laptop and MIDI controller along with me so I could get some practice in for an upcoming gig with another band I play with. I was therefore well-equipped to mess around with extra parts, and I think the change of scene helped my brain to work.

I'm such a sucker for big dramatic chords and long songs with sections, so the sound palette was immediately classic prog to go with the pre-existing Mellotron. I used a big polysynth key/pad sound straight out of Cheesecake, and a lead sound straight out of the '70s. The epic-sounding Em F# Bb chord progression in the opening section suited the big poly sound, but it repeats four times each time with no development. I figured something should appear once the pattern had established, which was the impetus for the little synth riff. It's the 'kids can be so cruel' melody from the end of the song wound back around to the start, in an attempt to retroactively develop the theme by putting it before itself. This notion was taken further by putting it in two separate places in the opening track of the album.

Then there's a big strumming and singing section in which keys felt obtrusive, so I joined the rhythm guitar part on uke, making my non-keys debut (I am not very good at the instrument so I predict very little uke will by forthcoming in the near future). This section eventually got some keys, mostly doing fills during the B section. In the transition from the strumming/singing section into the final uptempo part of the song, the synths from the opening come back quietly in a 'buckle up, here we bloody go' sort of way.

The ending section features the band riffing between D minor and Bb major for quite a while, and then continuing to do so when the vocals come in. To give the section more of a sense of building towards the vocals, I played 16th-note arpeggiated chords over the top: Dm over all the Dm bits, and then a progression of Dm, Am, F, Gm over the Bb bits. I reeeaaaally like the effect this has, increasing the tension until Alex comes in with his big vocal bits. The last full song on the album, No Replacing, had me feeling a little left out as everyone else is absolutely going for it in 10/8 while I'm holding down simple chords for full bars. A highly necessary part, but I felt like a freeloader. That's why my part at the end of The Little Billy Chronicles is so constantly busy- I want to play fast and difficult things too, damnit!

Evil Eyebrows

One of the first songs I learnt for the band when I joined, my main extra thing in this song was very similar to Billy: adding an extra chord progression to the outro so it wasn't just static riffing on Em for ages. The additional chords build upwards, too: Em, F#m, G, A, Em, F#, G, A. I like going major on the F# the second time around, it adds a little more clashiness.

The other thing I thought would be great were funhouse screams, malevolent laughter and gleeful cackles during the first, slow common time section. During overdubs, Alex and I took turns in front of the vocal mic contributing witch cackles, supervillain laughter/proclamations, and ghostly moaning. It turns out they were indeed great.
